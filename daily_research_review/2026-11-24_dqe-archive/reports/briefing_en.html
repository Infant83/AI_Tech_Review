A molecular surrogate uses similarities between previously calculated molecules and new ones to replace expensive calculations. A quantum kernel defines those similarities through overlaps of quantum states. This Daily Quantum Exercise asks whether changing similarity helps prediction, and whether measuring all the required similarities still saves computation.

Try the problem first

Open page 1 of the problem PDF. Solutions begin on page 2. The 15-minute fictional case predicts the hole reorganization energy of OLED transport materials under a specified DFT procedure. Hole reorganization energy describes the energetic cost of rearranging molecular structure when its charge state changes. The prediction target is the DFT label, rather than device lifetime or mobility.

Research connecting virtual screening, quantum chemistry, ML, synthesis and device tests in OLED development provides an industrial context for such surrogates.[1] The firm, data, circuit size and timings here are separate educational assumptions. Kernel ridge regression (KRR) solves a classical linear system using a regularized matrix of similarities. The official precomputed-kernel interface accepts a training–training matrix and a new-sample–training matrix separately.[2] Regression remains classical in the quantum route too.

Havlíček and colleagues used a superconducting processor for classification and kernel estimation in quantum feature spaces.[3] This exercise extends the kernel concept to an educational molecular regression case. An actual comparison could fix the DFT procedure, molecular descriptors and train/test split, fitting preprocessing and tuning models within training data. Testing error on unseen scaffolds or later-arriving molecules would help determine which future calculations can be entrusted to the surrogate. This is a proposed validation approach; no molecular dataset or QPU was run for this issue.

Changing similarity changes the prediction

Fixing the training mean at m=0.20 eV gives centered labels (−0.10, 0.10) eV. This direction has eigenvalue 1 under K+λI, so α=(−0.10, 0.10) eV. Substituting the two kernel vectors into r_pred=m+kᵀα gives 0.14 eV for the quantum kernel and 0.18 eV for the classical kernel. Absolute errors against the hidden 0.17 eV label are 0.030 eV and 0.010 eV respectively.

The classical prediction is closer on this point. The deployment criterion, independent-test MAE≤0.020 eV, requires errors across multiple unused molecules. Refitting the mean or regularization λ using the new point's true label would remove its independence as a test. Kernels and λ are dimensionless; α, predictions and errors have units of eV.

Audit kernel entries as a matrix

The separate audit matrix B has determinant 1−0.8²−0.8²=−0.28 and minimum eigenvalue 1−√1.28≈−0.1314. It is not positive semidefinite (PSD). Individual entries within the probability range do not make it a consistent ideal fidelity Gram matrix. B belongs to a different batch from the preceding hand calculation.

Finite-shot error, drift and pair-specific corrections are possible causes; the determinant alone cannot identify one. Repeat measurements under common calibration conditions. If applying a PSD correction, record the changed spectrum, prediction error and extra time. B+0.5I has positive minimum eigenvalue approximately 0.3686, but ridge regularization does not establish the physical consistency of the original B. Keep hidden test labels out of correction and hyperparameter selection.

Count the measurements needed to retain accuracy

Fixing diagonal entries at one and using symmetry gives 200×199/2=19,900 training pairs, with 20×200=4,000 more for prediction. The total 23,900 pairs at 1,000 shots each requires 23,900,000 shots. At the assumed 5,000 shots/s, measurement takes 79 minutes 40 seconds. Adding five minutes of preprocessing and ten minutes for other work gives 94 minutes 40 seconds end to end. The assumed classical route takes six minutes.

Quantum operational deployment is a No-Go under these assumptions because it exceeds the 30-minute limit. The classical route can be adopted after confirming independent-test accuracy, while a quantum Pivot could use a bounded, separately funded proof of concept. Keeping 15 minutes of fixed work leaves 900 seconds for measurement. The same shot count requires roughly 26,556 shots/s or more; at the present rate, shots per pair must fall to at most 188. These are arithmetic thresholds, not measured hardware capabilities.

The maximum binomial standard error √(0.25/1,000)≈0.0158 at 1,000 shots is dimensionless error in a kernel entry. Propagate noise in both training and prediction kernels through regression to assess prediction error in eV. A follow-up shot-reduction study should count accuracy changes alongside compilation, queues, communication, mitigation and retries. Classical simulation or emulation of the fixed eight-qubit circuit is another comparison; qubit count alone cannot establish a cost advantage.

Use tuned RBF/Laplacian kernel regression and tree ensembles on the same descriptors as strong classical baselines. Richer molecular representations can be additional challengers, with differences in inputs, tuning budgets and splits recorded. Omitting all 20 new DFT jobs gives an upper saving of 80 CPU·h. A batch that computes all 20 validation labels still incurs that cost. Assess net savings in later batches by subtracting residual audit and surrogate costs from jobs actually omitted. Distinguish CPU·h from elapsed time under parallel execution.
