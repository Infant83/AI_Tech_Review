When calculating the electronic state of a lithium atom, one can reshape the orbital functions rather than add more orbitals. Deb and Prasannaa's MSTO study adjusts Gaussian exponents and contraction coefficients, obtaining lower classical electronic-structure energies with the same orbital count as conventional minimal bases. Lithium also improves on some larger standard bases. This changes the representation before loading electrons into a circuit.[1]

What that small basis represents well depends on the system. Functions optimized for atomic energies did not transfer uniformly to molecules; hydrogen and diatomic carbon exposed limitations. Changing the basis also changes the finite-basis Hamiltonian, so a smaller circuit needs to be assessed alongside the error in the desired property. Extending this approach to excited states or energy differences in OLED molecules would require tests against those properties.[1]

The circuit estimates need the same care. The lithium VQE comparison counts gates for one ansatz preparation, while the reported energies are classical FCI references, not circuit outputs. Measurement and repetitions must be added before those gate savings become the cost of an energy estimate. This carries the input-preparation and target-accuracy questions in “Designing the full cost of quantum computation” back to basis selection.[1, 2] A compact representation is valuable when it supports the required answer at the required accuracy, alongside its qubit savings.
