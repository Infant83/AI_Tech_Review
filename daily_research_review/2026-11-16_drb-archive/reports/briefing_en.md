Testing more superconducting qubits also means bringing more wires into a refrigerator. Each line carries a useful signal while providing a path for heat and electromagnetic noise. Output lines are particularly demanding: amplifiers and circulators must recover very weak signals returning from the qubits. Routing several devices through a shared measurement path could reduce this hardware, provided the routing switch is itself sufficiently quiet. Krinner and colleagues explain why cryogenic wiring must balance heat conduction, signal dissipation and incoming thermal photons.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>]</span>

Fallik and colleagues operated a four-way RF multiplexer, fabricated in 22 nm FDSOI, at 10 mK. Its static consumption was 200 pW at a 0.55 V supply, with less than 2 dB insertion loss and more than 30 dB port isolation across DC–8 GHz. The switch preserves much of the selected signal while suppressing leakage into unselected ports. In separate experiments, the team connected it to transmon input and output lines without an additional attenuator between the multiplexer and the qubits. Output-line measurements yielded median energy-relaxation times T1 of 100–200 µs and Hahn-echo coherence times T2e of 50–100 µs.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-2">2</a>]</span>

That direct connection is an important part of the result. Earlier work drove qubits through a cryo-CMOS multiplexer with suitable thermal filtering.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-3">3</a>]</span> An output line cannot readily accept extra attenuation that would also weaken the returning signal, so the switch must generate little noise of its own. In the new input-line comparison, three qubits acquired additional dephasing rates of 2.3±0.4, 5.6±0.4 and 2.4±0.7 kHz (reported mean differences ± standard errors). Quantifying that change while retaining access to qubit coherence on the order of 100 µs supports sharing expensive output paths for sequential device characterization.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-2">2</a>]</span>

The 200 pW figure applies while a route is held. Changing ports costs 108 fJ per switching event. Applying that reported energy gives illustrative additional dynamic powers of 216 pW at 2,000 events per second and 108 nW at one million events per second. A static flux-bias current creates another heat load. At 0.23 mA, the paper estimates about 0.28 µW using a simulated on-state resistance; in that bias experiment, the mixing-chamber temperature rose by up to 5 mK and T1 fell by about 30%. Slowly selecting devices, rapidly changing routes and continuously applying current therefore require different heat budgets.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-2">2</a>]</span>

The immediate opportunity is sequential characterization of many quantum devices. When comparing qubit variability or screening materials and fabrication conditions, sharing the readout path is useful if individual device properties remain measurable. A follow-up review could examine which measurement schedules benefit most by considering wire count together with measurement time, switching energy and output noise.
