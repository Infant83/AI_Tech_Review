Fitting a molecule into a small quantum circuit requires choosing which electronic states to retain. Fixing some degrees of freedom reduces the register, but can also remove couplings between retained and excluded states. The energy contributed by those couplings helps determine how much accuracy a smaller register can deliver.

Stabilizer-subspace perturbation theory (SSPT), proposed by Yao, Liu, and Li, uses discarded couplings to construct a correction. Its starting method, CS-VQE, optimizes a state within a smaller space by fixing the eigenvalues of stabilizer operators. Those constraints need not be exact symmetries of the full Hamiltonian. Pauli operators eliminated by the projection can therefore identify connections to excluded states. SSPT uses them to build an external space and evaluates its coupling to the reference through second-order perturbation theory. The required matrix elements factor into expectation values on the reduced register and analytic contributions from the fixed degrees of freedom.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>]</span>

The authors tested this construction with classical statevectors, without finite-shot sampling or device noise. For LiH with one contextual qubit, the absolute energy error relative to full configuration interaction (FCI) fell from 5.65 to 0.06 mHa. In that single-geometry benchmark, one contextual qubit then met the 1.6 mHa threshold that required four without the correction. This is the size of a reduced register within a chosen orbital space, rather than a hardware demonstration of an entire molecule on one qubit.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>]</span>

Stretching a bond also exposes where the correction becomes unstable. In the two-contextual-qubit LiH calculation at 2.9 Å, an external state approached the reference energy. The resulting small denominator drove the error to 13.37 mHa. Treating that state together with the reference by diagonalization, or expanding the register to three or four qubits, mitigated the spike. This example helps decide when a strongly mixing state belongs in the space solved explicitly rather than in a small perturbative correction.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>]</span>

The two-spin example in our earlier VQE review explained why increasing shots cannot repair the omission of states coupled to a selected subspace.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-2">2</a>]</span> SSPT adds a concrete way to reuse the discarded couplings. A useful next comparison would adapt the register and external space along a bond coordinate, then measure the cost of reaching the same energy-difference accuracy, including expectation-value acquisition and classical matrix operations. That comparison would help determine which correlations should remain in the quantum state and which can be handled by a correction.
