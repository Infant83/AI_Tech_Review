Packing electron qubits into a small area also means designing the electrodes that confine them and deliver their control signals. Li and colleagues tested a route that uses confinement at a solid-neon surface to reduce that overhead.[1] Quantum information resides in an electron's motional states above the neon. A separate single-electron study published in 2024 reported relaxation and coherence times on the order of 0.1 ms.[2] The new work addresses the next task: bringing neighboring electrons into interaction and controlling their joint dynamics.

The two-electron experiment gives the resonator a bright partner and a dark one. Their dipole orientations allow Qb to couple appreciably to the microwave resonator while Qd has almost no detectable direct coupling. Charge–charge interaction connects the two. Spectroscopy gives a coupling J/(2π)=3.35(9) MHz, above a hybrid linewidth of 0.6(1) MHz. With a shared microwave drive and one d.c. bias, the researchers measured time-domain oscillations associated with CR and bSWAP operations.[1]

Reading the dark electron also involves the bright one. A resonator probe pulse ac-Stark shifts the bright qubit into a condition that exchanges their excited-state populations, transferring information about the dark electron to the bright-qubit signal. The experiment tracks this operation through a normalized resonator I quadrature. The authors' simulations include both the drive and the readout process to explain the measured patterns. The separately presented gate fidelities are simulated values, rather than experimental process-fidelity measurements.[1]

A separate three-electron spectroscopy experiment reported J12/(2π)=62.5(5) MHz. It used a different cooldown and a Nb resonator, whereas the two-electron operations used TiN. Read together, the experiments make neon confinement and dipole orientation part of the control interface, alongside the wiring and resonator. A useful next design question is how reproducibly a surface structure sets those couplings, and how much variation shared driving and calibrated readout can accommodate.[1]
