Two optical paths make a convenient qubit on a photonic chip: compact interferometers can prepare and measure their superposition. Outside the chip, temperature and vibration change the relative phase of those paths. Preserving that relationship becomes harder over a long link. Roux and colleagues mapped the chip’s path encoding directly onto two fiber cores and distributed entanglement between silicon chips separated by an 80-km fiber link.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>]</span>

A classical reference traveling alongside the quantum signal provides the correction. Two spiral waveguides on the transmitter generate photon pairs. One photon of each pair is measured locally; the other travels to the receiver. Residual pump light traverses the same two cores and acquires the relative phase fluctuations. At the receiver, a photodiode reads its interference, and a phase-locked loop drives an external fiber stretcher. The setup therefore obtains a reference without an additional laser. Correlated environmental noise in the two cores and active feedback work together to hold the relative phase.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>]</span>

After transmission through an 80-km dual-core fiber spool in the laboratory, reconstructed photon-pair states had a Bell-state fidelity of 85.7±0.2%. Using measured error rates, the authors calculated a BBM92 secret-key rate of 2.03 bit/s in the infinite-key limit. Finite datasets must also allocate events to estimating errors. For a block of 100,000 detected events, their finite-key calculation gives 0.71 bit/s with an estimated accumulation time of about four hours. That time is inferred from the detection rate, rather than a record of a completed long-duration key-collection run.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>]</span>

The experiment shows that path-encoded entanglement can connect chips across a long fiber link without converting to another encoding. Coupling between the chips and fiber accounts for a substantial part of the remaining loss, at roughly 7 dB per facet. Better couplers and faster measurement-basis switching therefore connect materials and device development directly to link throughput. A follow-up review could trace how many generated pairs survive coupling, transmission, stabilization, and detection, reading state quality alongside the useful transmission rate.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>]</span>

The work had also been presented at conferences before journal publication. The QPIC1550 consortium’s October 8 ECOC report highlights the same 80-km result, 85.7% fidelity, and phase-locking scheme. We treat it as the research consortium’s account; the apparatus and key-rate conditions come from the primary paper.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-2">2</a>]</span>
