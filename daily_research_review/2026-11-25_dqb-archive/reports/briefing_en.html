An iron–sulphur cluster can have zero total spin while the two parts on either side of an orbital partition carry large spins. Preserving how those spins couple can reduce both the stored description of the electronic wavefunction and the circuit cost of preparing the same state. The spin-adapted preparation method of Sims-Goh and colleagues carries a compression already used in classical electronic-structure calculations into the quantum circuit.[1]

A matrix product state (MPS) obtained with the density matrix renormalisation group (DMRG) describes the electronic state by connecting orbitals in sequence. Instead of treating every component of a spin multiplet separately, reduced tensors and known spin-coupling rules retain the relations between them. The authors relabel the reduced tensors for an existing block-sparse loader, then apply an orbital-by-orbital rotation determined by Clebsch–Gordan coefficients to recover the state in the occupation basis. In the two iron–sulphur examples, these basis transforms account for less than 0.2% of the preparation circuit’s Toffoli count.[1]

For loading the same DMRG state of the [Fe₄S₄]²⁻ model, the logical Toffoli count is 135 times smaller than the dense baseline and 4.9 times smaller than the block-sparse baseline using electron number and spin projection. These resource estimates use 15-bit rotation angles and minimise the Toffoli count without an ancilla cap. Both baseline protocols are costed as published; the dense baseline omits a later saving for real-valued tensors that the block-sparse baseline includes. Translating the reductions into physical execution time or a speedup of the complete energy calculation therefore requires a further cost model.[1]

Our earlier “VQE Today and Beyond” review discussed the importance of overlap with the target eigenstate when connecting good initial-state preparation to precise energy readout.[2] The new result removes redundancy in loading that state into a circuit. The paper’s truncation fidelities are measured against a larger reference MPS, rather than the exact ground state, and its [Fe₄S₄]²⁻ DMRG calculation did not reach the specified energy-convergence threshold. Assessing cheaper preparation requires following the state through the repetitions needed for successful quantum phase estimation (QPE) and the costs of time evolution and error correction. Symmetry is valuable when its compact representation survives all the way to use.
