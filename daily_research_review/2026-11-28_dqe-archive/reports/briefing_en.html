Wireless access points sharing a channel can contend for airtime or retransmit packets. Choosing channels calls for both an interference model and attention to clients with low throughput. This Daily Quantum Exercise draws four access points in a warehouse and evaluates the graph’s preferred assignment against operating requirements for the next shift.

Formal references and production information follow the three original pages in a public-edition appendix. The warehouse, weights, throughput, times and diagrams are educational synthetic inputs.

Cisco’s RRM Release 8.5 documentation describes dynamic channel assignment intended to reduce contention among access points.[1] This exercise fixes power, bandwidth and client positions and permits only two channels. Its weighted interference sum fits a model discussed in ZIB’s frequency-assignment classification.[2] A practical evaluation could replay a new shift’s load separately from the data used to construct the graph, checking the fifth percentile of client throughput, P5, to include poorly served clients.

Expand the optimal assignment, time budget and operating decision

Examine the edge shared by two triangles

When B and C share a channel, the middle edge contributes five points. Assigning A and D to the other channel removes interference on the four outer edges. When B and C differ, A must share with one of them, costing at least three points; D also costs at least three. There is no A–D edge, so these choices are independent. The two minima are therefore five and six; one global optimum is (A,B,C,D)=(0,1,1,0).

Replacing only the middle weight by w changes the lower bounds to w and six. They tie at w=6; larger w favors separating B and C. Checking whether uncertainty in measured weights can cross this boundary would help assess assignment stability.

Translate the graph cost into bits and circuits

For channel bits x, F(x)=Σw(1−xᵢ−xⱼ+2xᵢxⱼ). With z=1−2x, F(z)=½Σw(1+zᵢzⱼ), summed over the five edges. Total weight is 19, making the weighted MaxCut value 19−F with maximum 14. Complementing every channel bit leaves F unchanged, so score enumeration can use eight representatives.

Before symmetry reduction, p=1 QAOA uses four problem qubits and five ZZ terms in its cost layer. The constant contributes a common phase, followed by an X mixer and classical angle optimization. Five logical ZZ terms must not be counted as five native two-qubit gates. Harrigan and colleagues studied MaxCut QAOA on Sycamore and compilation for its connectivity.[3] This AP exercise supplies no angles, optimal-state probability or circuit execution result.

Select a tested assignment within the operating clock

The classical path takes 0.8+0.1+0.2=1.1 seconds. QAOA’s ten evaluations, including final sampling, require 5,000 shots: 2.5 seconds at 2,000 shots/s. Adding four seconds of fixed costs gives 6.5 seconds. Holding the shots and other costs fixed leaves one second to meet the five-second limit, requiring at least 5,000 shots/s. The same prior P5 test is provided to both paths outside this operating clock.

The supplied synthetic replay results are F=5 and P5=18 Mbit/s for (0,1,1,0), versus F=6 and P5=22 Mbit/s for (0,0,1,1). Use the classical path for the second assignment today: it meets the 20 Mbit/s requirement among the two tested choices, although it does not minimize the original score. P5 is not supplied for other assignments or for complemented channel labels. Symmetry of the interference score does not determine a replay’s throughput.

QAOA operation is No-Go under the assumed timetable. A follow-up PoC could give the classical and quantum paths the same graph, candidate count, computing budget and held-out loads. Compare the controller’s existing automatic assignment under those same loads. For larger models, compare against multi-start local search and integer programming or branch-and-bound, recording best F, optimal-solution hit rate, field P5 and total time separately. Reconsider operation when at least 95% of repeated larger tests jointly meet the five-second and P5 requirements and improve cost or throughput. This is a proposed adoption criterion, not a measured success rate for this issue.

Figure 3 in the quantum-algorithms review explains how QAOA’s cost phases and mixer change a distribution over candidates. That example is an unweighted four-cycle. Extending a circuit experiment to this interference graph, with its shared edge and unequal weights, requires recalculating angles and distributions for the corresponding cost operator.

A channel-policy test on a new shift can apply that design by keeping weight-tuning and evaluation data separate and retaining client-level outcomes.
