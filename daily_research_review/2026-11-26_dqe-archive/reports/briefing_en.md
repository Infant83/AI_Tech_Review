Several suppliers can miss delivery together when they use the same logistics hub. This Daily Quantum Exercise puts that shared disruption into an emergency procurement decision at an electronic components factory. Calculating both expected shortage cost and the probability of receiving the entire order shows how the purchasing team’s service objective changes the comparison.

<h3>Evaluate today’s order in every scenario</h3>
<p>The binary variables a,b,c represent reservations from suppliers A,B,C made before tomorrow’s disruption is known. Choosing a different order after observing each scenario would answer a different decision problem. Gurobi’s Multiple Scenarios feature computes a separate optimal solution for each model variant; shared order decisions and probability constraints must be specified in the model.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-2">2</a>]</span></p>
<ul><li>AB: S0·S1·S2 shortages 0·4·2 boxes, expected total cost KRW 310,000, full-demand probability 70%.</li><li>AC: shortages 0·4·2 boxes, expected total cost KRW 330,000, full-demand probability 70%.</li><li>BC: shortages 0·0·8 boxes, expected total cost KRW 280,000, full-demand probability 90%.</li><li>ABC: shortages 0·0·2 boxes, expected total cost KRW 340,000, full-demand probability 90%.</li></ul>
<p>BC is optimal among BC and ABC, the policies satisfying the 90% full-demand requirement. Adding A reduces the S2 shortage by six boxes without filling demand. Expected shortage cost falls by KRW 60,000 while the reservation costs KRW 120,000, increasing total cost by KRW 60,000. AB’s expected fulfilled fraction is 87.5%, whereas its probability of filling the entire demand is 70%. Averaging partially fulfilled quantities measures a different service outcome.</p>
<h3>Keep both the quadratic cost and the delivery constraint</h3>
<p>At the base probabilities, F=80−36a−27b−25c+14ab+14ac+14abc contains a cubic term. Introducing y=ab replaces 14abc with 14yc and adds P(ab−2ay−2by+3y). For fixed a,b,c, an inconsistent y incurs at least P and can improve the other cost term by at most 14. Thus P&gt;14, for example 15, is sufficient to enforce this substitution strictly. F and P here are in units of KRW 10,000. This is a sufficient bound, not a claim of the smallest necessary penalty.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-3">3</a>]</span></p>
<p>The supplied substitution uses four binary variables a,b,c,y, excluding additional service-constraint variables and physical qubits. A MILP can introduce shortage u and binary violation allowance z for each scenario, with u≥8−q, u≥0, q+8z≥8 and Σp z≤0.10. Since q≥0, big-M=8 boxes suffices. A positive shortage cost makes u equal the actual shortage at an optimum; every scenario shares the same order variables. Encoding these constraints in a QUBO also requires counting violation variables, slack encoding and penalties.</p>
<p>Constraint preprocessing makes this particular example simpler. S0 and S1 each have probability greater than 0.10, so both must meet demand. S1 forces b=c=1, reducing the cost to F=28+6a and fixing a=0. Applying this preprocessing equally to both solvers leaves no nontrivial search. Four bits describe the given substitution, not a minimum resource count over every possible formulation.</p>
<h3>Calculate operating time and the limits of the available choices</h3>
<p>1,000 reads×20 μs equals 0.020 seconds. The full annealing path takes 2+8+15+4+0.020+1=30.020 seconds; the classical path takes 2+0.30+1=3.30 seconds. Under the assumed 10-second limit, classical operation is Go for the base model and quantum operation is No-Go. Even instantaneous annealing would leave 30 seconds of the other assumed costs.</p>
<p>Annealing samples low-energy solutions of a quadratic objective. QAOA implements the Ising cost in circuits and repeats measurements and classical parameter optimization. Model choices such as BQM and CQM are distinct from classical, QPU and hybrid sampler choices.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-4">4</a>]</span> A hardware comparison would record embedding, chain lengths, chain breaks and coefficient precision for annealing, or routed depth, two-qubit gates, shots and optimization iterations for QAOA. Queueing, communication, decoding, validation and retries belong in the total time.</p>
<p>Under stress, S2 delivers only six boxes even when all suppliers are booked. Its probability is 25%, so the full-demand probability is bounded above by 75%. The current choices cannot satisfy the 90% constraint, calling for a Pivot. Two guaranteed boxes from a separate hub are one possible extension whose cost and feasibility would need evaluation. Changing scenario probabilities also requires recomputing the expected-cost polynomial.</p>
<p>A follow-up PoC could use enumeration or MILP with the same choices, scenarios and preprocessing as its baseline. For a larger model, check a gap≤1% against a MILP lower bound, the original service probability≥90%, and full time≤10 seconds, then compare time or cost at an equal total budget. Include failures and timeouts in the repeated-run denominator. Calibrating probabilities against shared-disruption observations from a held-out period and reporting stress shortages would help determine where a procurement policy can be used. This is a proposed evaluation, not an experiment run for this issue.</p>
