What must we know about a quantum device before entrusting it with the next computation? Learning a model that works for every possible input demands different information from identifying one of a few candidate states and choosing the next operation. A channel-learning theorem, a neutral-atom feedback experiment and a calculation of erbium defects make those goals concrete. Published online on November 12, 10 and 3, respectively, these are newly examined papers in today's brief.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>–<a href="#ref-3">3</a>]</span>

Mele and Bittel establish the query cost of learning an entire noisy quantum process. Their error criterion requires the estimated channel to remain close to the true one even when arbitrary inputs and auxiliary systems are allowed. At fixed accuracy, they match the dimensional dependence of the upper and lower bounds in the input dimension, output dimension and Kraus rank. For a general d-dimensional channel, the dependence improves from the previously known roughly d⁶ to d⁴. The unknown channel is queried in parallel; the resulting state copies then undergo joint quantum purification and classical model reconstruction. This fixes an information-cost benchmark for learning the full process. The distinctions made in the earlier certification tutorial—total repetitions, measurement settings and classical processing—help explain where that benchmark applies.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-4">4</a>]</span>

Partial readout in an atomic array serves a different goal. Measuring and reusing an ancilla while protecting data atoms allows a computation to continue. Tian Xia's 2023 APS Physics commentary introduces this challenge.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-5">5</a>]</span> The 2026 experiment by Lee and colleagues combines rapid readout, optical shielding and real-time control in five Rb atoms coupled to an optical cavity. Given three identical copies of one of three nonorthogonal states, the scheme that selects the next measurement axis from an earlier result reports a discrimination probability of 81(2)%, compared with 73(2)% for the fixed-ZX scheme. Both schemes condition the final rotation on measurement outcomes. The added adaptivity lies in selecting the second measurement axis. A 45 μs measurement-and-processing cycle is also distinct from the approximately 300 μs duration of the full circuit.

The first-principles study of Er:CaWO₄ takes the question inside the material. A 2023 Nature Research Briefing describes the motivation for choosing a host with sparse nuclear spins and a nonpolar site to reduce optical frequency fluctuations.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-6">6</a>]</span> The new hybrid-DFT work asks where implanted erbium can settle and how it can rearrange among nearby defects. The calculated barrier for displacing Ca and occupying its site is 0.94 eV; direct migration between interstitial sites has a barrier of 2.32 eV. These are different pathways. They make site occupancy, charge compensation and processing history concrete design variables for a stable emitter. The calculation does not directly predict spin-coherence time or memory efficiency, and CaWO₄ is a different host from the Er:Y₂SiO₅ discussed previously.

The common question is which properties must be known to accomplish the intended task. Channel-query adaptivity can be unnecessary for optimal dimensional scaling in full-process tomography while adaptive measurement axes improve a particular state-discrimination experiment. Both statements can hold because the targets and error criteria differ. Specifying the goal makes the required measurements, quantum memory, control latency and material calculations more concrete.
