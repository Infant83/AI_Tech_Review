An error-correction check can involve more data qubits than an ancilla has immediate neighbours. Directional codes, introduced by Gehér and colleagues, exploit the exchange of quantum states in an iSWAP-based circuit. The devices remain fixed on a square grid while the ancilla state encounters different data states in successive layers. The code and the circuit that extracts its syndrome are designed together.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>]</span>

The paper reports a promising gain in circuit-noise simulations of a memory storing twelve logical qubits. At physical error rate 0.001 with the Tesseract short-beam decoder, a fit across code sizes gives the rotated directional family a physical-qubit resource ratio of 29.2% relative to the rotated toric code. This is a fitted result for a memory with toroidal connections. Implementing the boundary on a planar chip and decoding fast enough remain separate tasks, both identified as future work in the paper.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-1">1</a>]</span>

Materials inverse design also benefits from separating what must be learned anew from what can be reused. The separate EGMOF study connects a target property to 183 descriptors, then uses a pretrained module to generate metal–organic framework (MOF) structures. The structure module has already learned from about 500,000 hypothetical frameworks. A test using 1,000 new property-labelled examples therefore does not mean the whole system learned from 1,000 structures. Its 90% hydrogen-uptake hit rate is likewise a computational benchmark with a tolerance defined by the training distribution's standard deviation. This classical materials-AI workflow uses electronic-structure labels through QMOF; it is not a new QPU computation.<span class="citation">[<a href="#ref-2">2</a>]</span>

A proposed error-correction review would connect the fitted qubit savings to planar wiring, syndrome-cycle time and real-time decoding costs. Directional codes appeared online on 25 November and were already introduced in DQB 26; today's brief adds primary-source reading. EGMOF appeared online on 28 November. The [Fock-space preparation and sensing review](https://infant83.io/AI_Tech_Review/reviews/2026-11-28_fock-space-state-preparation-readout/en/), completed today, remains a separate article.
