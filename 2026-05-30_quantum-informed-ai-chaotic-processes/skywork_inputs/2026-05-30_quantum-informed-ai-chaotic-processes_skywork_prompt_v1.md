# Skywork Prompt v1 - Quantum-Informed AI for Chaotic Processes

Create a Korean technical review deck for engineers and research leaders.

## Deck Goal

Explain why `Quantum-Informed AI for Chaotic Processes` is worth tracking, what the 2026 QIML paper actually demonstrates, how it relates to quantum reservoir computing, and what questions remain before treating it as practical engineering technology.

## Audience

- AI/ML engineers
- simulation/HPC researchers
- product and research leaders evaluating emerging AI technologies

## Source Base

- `reports/2026-05-30_quantum-informed-ai-chaotic-processes_deepresearch.md`
- `reports/2026-05-30_quantum-informed-ai-chaotic-processes_final_review.md`

## Visual Style

- Clean technical editorial style.
- White or near-white background, deep gray text, restrained accent color.
- Avoid decorative quantum-glow imagery.
- Use diagrams, evaluation matrices, and paper maps.

## Slide Outline

1. Title: `양자 prior는 혼돈 예측을 얼마나 안정화할 수 있을까`
2. Video signal: where the topic came from and what needs verification.
3. Problem: chaotic prediction fails differently from ordinary regression.
4. QIML architecture: chaotic data -> quantum generator -> Q-Prior -> classical Koopman predictor.
5. What the 2026 paper shows: datasets, metrics, reported improvements, hardware note.
6. Paper map: classical reservoir computing, QRC, RF-QRC, small-qubit forecasting, edge of many-body chaos.
7. Evaluation checklist: metrics, baselines, hardware cost, replication.
8. Recommendation: track as Q-Prior/statistical-regularization direction, not as generic quantum-AI breakthrough.

## Required Caveats

- Do not say the paper directly evaluated disease spread.
- Do not flatten `up to 17.25%` and `up to 29.36%` into a generic 20% accuracy claim.
- Do not imply a general quantum advantage has been proven across all chaotic forecasting tasks.
- Keep `QIML`, `QRC`, `Q-Prior`, `Koopman`, `Lyapunov time`, and `invariant measure` technically distinct.
